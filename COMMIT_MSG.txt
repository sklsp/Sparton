feat(saas): signup, verification, password reset, Stripe billing, plan limits

Phase 4. Turns the product from "a thing you can run" into "a thing you can
sell".

Signup and account recovery
- Email verification via a single auth_tokens table (D-011), tokens stored
  HASHED like session tokens, single-use, re-issuing invalidates the previous
  one, and a verification link cannot be redeemed as a password reset.
- Enforced in production only, and at the PRODUCT boundary rather than at
  signup: a customer who loses the email must still be able to sign in and
  resend, or they are locked out of their own account with no recovery.
- Password reset and change-password. A reset REVOKES EVERY SESSION, because
  the reset may have been prompted by a compromise.
- forgot-password always answers identically for known and unknown addresses.
- Email over stdlib SMTP; with no SMTP configured it writes data/outbox/*.eml,
  so local dev and CI exercise the real flow with no mail server and no network.

Billing, with no new dependency (D-002)
- app/billing/plans.py: Free (1 shop/3 competitors), Pro EUR29 (3/15),
  Business EUR79 (10/50). get_plan() FAILS CLOSED to Free on an unknown id,
  so a bad row can never produce an unlimited allowance.
- check_shops / check_competitors / check_tokens raise 402 with a
  machine-readable plan_limit_reached detail. check_crawl_frequency CLAMPS
  rather than refusing: the customer asked for something valid.
- Every count filters on an explicit organization_id. Billing deliberately
  does NOT use TenantContext.scoped, which is unscoped for machine principals.
- The plan is resolved SERVER-SIDE on every mutating route. The browser never
  decides what plan it is on.
- app/billing/stripe.py: Checkout, Customer Portal, customer creation, and
  webhook signature verification (constant-time HMAC over the RAW body, with a
  replay window and multi-v1 rotation support).
- The webhook is the ONLY writer of paid plans (D-014), idempotent by event
  id, and a failure returns 500 so Stripe retries rather than silently
  diverging from what the customer paid for.
- An unknown Stripe price fails closed to Free rather than granting access.

Duplicate shop is reported as 409, not as a 402 upsell: telling someone to
upgrade when they have simply re-added a shop makes a limit feel arbitrary.

Two more bugs the tests caught: _flatten() was stringifying pre-built bracket
keys, and the new auth routes were registered as /auth/auth/... (the router
already carries the prefix), so all six 404'd.
